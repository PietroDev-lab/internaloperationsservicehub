title : ADR 001 : Storage Strategy for the Service Hub
--- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- -

# problem

- The product-spec.md requires robust data tracking : an employee request system , distinct status updates , HR responses , and an append-only audit history log .
- However , the architecture.md explicitly mandates the use of the company's existing database and strictly constraints the design to "Adapter over existing DB, no new schema" .
- We must find a way to persist structured relational data (Requests , History Logs , Notification Queues) safely without executing any Data Definition Language (DDL) operations (like CREATE TABLE) in the company database .

# options

*Option 1 : Overload Existing Ticketing/Notes Tables*
- If the company database already has tables for IT tickets or employee notes , we could map our Request and History fields into their existing columns .
- *Pros :* Uses existing relational structures .
- *Cons :* High risk of data corruption or unintended side effects if other company systems rely on those tables . We would have to force HR statuses into columns not meant for them .

*Option 2 : Serialize to JSON in an Existing Generic Extension Table*
- Most enterprise databases have a generic key-value , configuration , or app metadata table (such as : App_Data , Employee_Extensions) . We can store our Requests and Request_History_Logs as serialized JSONB/JSON strings inside one of these existing tables , using the employee ID as the reference key .
- *Pros :* Strictly obeys the "no new schema" constraint . Requires zero database migration or DBA approval . Keeps Service Hub data isolated from other core tables .
- *Cons :* Referential integrity (ensuring an employee exists) must be handled entirely in our Node/Express Engine . Searching or sorting by request status requires JSON extraction queries .

*Option 3 : Negotiate an Exception for Minimal Schema Additions*
- Push back on the constraint and request permission to add a dedicated service_hub namespace with three specific tables (Requests , Request_History , Notification_Queue) .
- *Pros :* Perfect referential integrity , performant indexing , and native SQL querying for the HR dashboard .
- *Cons :* Directly violates a stated architectural constraint . Might block the project if DB admins refuse .

# decision

- We will proceed with Option 2 : Serialize to JSON in an Existing Generic Extension Table .
- The Request Processing Engine will act as the adapter , converting our logical data model (Requests , Append-only History) into JSON documents and persisting them in an existing generic table .

# consequences

- *Positive :* We respect the strict "no new schema" boundary defined in the architecture . Development can proceed immediately without waiting for database migrations or DBA approvals .
- *Positive :* The system handles failures cleanly ; the Engine will handle all JSON schema validation internally before attempting to write , ensuring data safety .
- *Negative :* HR filtering and searching (such as : "Show me all Pending requests") will rely on database-level JSON querying (like JSON_EXTRACT) , which is historically slower than querying indexed columns .
- *Mitigation :* The non-functional requirements cap the system at an incredibly small scale : "50 concurrent employees" and "100 requests per hour" . At this low volume , the performance penalty of JSON querying is entirely negligible and will easily satisfy the "processed within 2 seconds" requirement .
